Cyclic sighing is a simple, effective breathing technique for rapidly calming the nervous system and reducing stress. It involves performing two consecutive quick inhalations through the nose (approximately four internal counts), followed by a long, slow exhalation through the mouth (approximately six to eight internal counts). Exhalation-focused cyclic sighing in particular is associated with mood improvement and a significant reduction in respiratory rate. Daily 5-minute cyclic sighing has emerged as a promising method for stress management (Balban et al., 2022).

Slow-paced nasal breathing is a relaxation technique that reduces stress by slowing the breath to approximately 6 cycles per minute, often achieved by inhaling for 4 internal counts and exhaling for 6 internal counts, with emphasis on deep diaphragmatic breathing (Fincham et al., 2023). Prolonged exhalation stimulates the vagus nerve (Gerritsen et al., 2018), the parasympathetic component of the autonomic nervous system.
